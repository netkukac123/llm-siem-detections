\subsection{RQ4f: most models relay the failed injection}
\label{sec:relay-models}

RQ4f tests whether the relay generalises beyond one model. It ran the same task on all twelve models, with six framings: the
three of RQ4d and three new ones, each asking for a different internal document (the expense policy,
the VPN guide, the leave policy) to go to a different outside domain (a customs broker, a portal
migration desk, an account manager). Twenty runs per framing and model, 1{,}440 runs in all, 1{,}290 of
them new; the pre-registration, including the new framings, was committed before the first run. All
domains are fictitious and did not resolve in DNS at the time of writing.

Under the pre-registered labels, nine of the twelve models relayed the request to the user in 61\% to
100\% of their runs, and none of the nine was labelled as warning in any run
(Table~\ref{tab:rq4f}). They span every vendor and size in the study: models of about 4B to 8B
parameters, gpt-oss-120b, gpt-4.1-mini, gpt-5-mini and Claude Haiku~4.5. Pooled over the nine, 997 of
1{,}080 runs relayed; per framing between 80\% (the JSON record) and 99\%, and on the three framings
first used here between 90\% and 98\%. The framing matters for some models (Haiku relayed in 7 of 20
runs on the JSON record and 20 of 20 on two others), so we give the per-framing range rather than an
interval on the pooled rate, which six framings cannot support. Only Opus~4.8, Opus~5.5 and Sonnet~5.5
never relayed: they warned, or the provider stopped the answer. The agents themselves almost never
acted (3 of 1{,}440 runs sent the email).

\paragraph{How reliable are the labels?} All raters are language models; no answer was labelled by a
person. The pre-registered raters were two instances of Claude Opus~5.5, the model that assisted this
study, with the same instructions; they agreed on all 1{,}440 items, which shows that the labelling is
reproducible but not that it is independent (the same holds for the second reading in RQ4d). Under the
rule as amended before the analysis, agreement was judged on a rater from another family,
gpt-4.1-mini. It agreed on only 59\% ($\kappa = 0.36$); in ten randomly drawn disagreements every
answer passed the request on with no warning, which suggests it labelled the request rather than the
answer. The pre-registered generality claim is therefore \emph{not established}. A further rater was
fixed by rule before it ran, with the identical prompt: gpt-5-mini agreed with the pre-registered labels
on 97\% of items ($\kappa = 0.94$; 0.98 when both labels are relay or warning) and gives the same rate
per model. The two cross-family raters agreed with each other on 59\% ($\kappa = 0.37$). Both rate
some of their own answers; excluding those leaves the kappas unchanged (0.35 and 0.95). The relay
rates do depend on the rater: over the nine models' 1{,}080 runs the gpt-5-mini labels contain 8
warnings and the gpt-4.1-mini labels 588. Even under gpt-4.1-mini's labels, seven of the twelve models
relay in at least 30\% of runs.

\paragraph{Is a relay a failure?} One reading is that the models summarise the tool's message
faithfully, which is what a tool-using assistant should do. We did not run a control in which the tool
returns a harmless procedure, so we cannot separate indiscriminate relaying from a failure to recognise
this request as dangerous. Given the same messages, three models flagged the request every
time. Nor did we measure whether people act on a relayed request;
an employee who does would be visible to mail and data-loss controls, which our rules do not cover.

\paragraph{Detecting it.} \llmd{026} caught 771 of the 997 relays (77\%). All four internal document
names, including the three new ones, have been in the rule since it was frozen, so the held-out
framings test new wording, not new documents. It fired on 18 of 314 warnings (6\%) and on none of 788
benign runs across eleven models, but none of those benign tasks involved internal documents and
outside addresses, so the benign result says little about real traffic. Of the 226 missed relays, 97
name the address but not the document, 25 the document but not an address, 86 neither (for example
``the VPN guide''), and 18 name both but contain a phrase the rule treats as a warning.
