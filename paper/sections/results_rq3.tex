\subsection{RQ3: rules and ML classifiers}
\label{sec:rq3}

On the clean datasets, pooled per surface (Table~\ref{tab:rq3-pooled}), ProtectAI's DeBERTa
classifier recalls 82.7\% of attack prompts at a 3.8\% false-positive rate on real benign prompts,
against 4.5\% recall and a 0.01\% false-positive rate (3 of 27{,}948) for the prompt rules. On injected
documents and poisoned tool descriptions every detector is weak: DeBERTa recalls 26.6\% of injected
documents (5.7\% FPR) and 26.8\% of poisoned tool descriptions (1.6\% FPR); Prompt Guard~2 recalls
3.7\% of injected documents. The rules and DeBERTa overlap little on tool descriptions, so their union
reaches 44.7\% at 1.8\% FPR, the only surface where combining them pays.

The classifiers are the better input filter for direct prompts, and neither they nor the rules see
indirect injection in documents well. That is the gap the behavioural rules are meant to cover:
they do not read the injected text at all.
