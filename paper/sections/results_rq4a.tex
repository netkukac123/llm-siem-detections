\subsection{RQ4 Part A: rewording}
\label{sec:rq4a}

Of 300 real attack prompts that the rules detect, 1\% are still detected after a synonym swap and 8\%
after a paraphrase (Table~\ref{tab:rq4-offline}); the rules survive base64 wrapping only because one
rule looks for base64 blobs as such. The classifiers survive character-level operators (DeBERTa 93\%
to 100\%) and degrade under semantic rewriting (58\% on paraphrase, 63\% to 73\% on translation).
Even the union of rules and both classifiers catches 61\% of paraphrased seeds. The seeds are
clustered (70\% come from one near-duplicate family), but the collapse is the same in both main seed
datasets. The operators are fixed and receive no feedback from the detectors, so a real attacker does
at least this well.
