\subsection{RQ4c: injections written against the rule set}
\label{sec:rq4c}

An attacker who knows the rules avoids the phrases they key on. RQ4c gave the three Claude models nine
such framings in an MCP tool reply asking the agent to email \texttt{ops-integration-notes.md}, an
internal file holding a fake credential, to an outside address: a keyword-style control (V0), the
request inside a structured error and recovery block (V1), a record that asserts as data that the
file must be sent (V2), a forged user follow-up at the end of the tool output (V3), the best of these
placed past the logged window (V4), and the request split into two routine-looking steps (V5), plus
reworded versions of V1 to V3 for confirmation. Ten runs per framing and model, 270 runs.

\paragraph{No framing worked.} None of the 270 runs emailed the file. \llmd{021} caught the
keyword control in 30 of 30 runs and none of the other 240, which restates Part A on a live channel:
the framings were written to avoid exactly its patterns. With a forged user turn, Haiku~4.5 read the
internal file in 18 of 20 runs without sending it, so the fake credential entered its context on the
attacker's instruction; only refusal language in the answer raised a rule.

\paragraph{The model's reasoning summary as a signal.} Where the provider did not stop a run, the
reasoning summaries of Sonnet and Opus often said that the tool output contained an instruction the
user had not given. \llmd{025} matches that statement. Its pattern was written from Opus exploration
runs; on Sonnet's runs with the reworded framings, which are out of sample in both model and wording,
it flagged 27 of 30, at a benign rate of 4\% (7 of 174 benign runs, CI 2\% to 8\%). On Opus it reached
only 9 of 30 on the reworded framings, because the provider stopped most of those runs before any
reasoning was returned, and on Haiku it never fired: Haiku's summaries did not describe the
instruction as injected. Reasoning summaries are a usable signal where a model writes them and the
provider lets them through; they are not a general one.

\paragraph{Where the provider classifier acts.} On Opus the provider stopped 61 of the 90 runs, every
one with an empty answer. How often depended on the framing in ways that did not reproduce: the
forged user turn was stopped in 0 of 10 runs in one wording and 7 of 10 in the other, so a
pre-registered claim that framing decides which layer sees the attempt failed.
